How the saving is worked out

Monthly kWh is backed out of the spend through the tariff's price blocks. Solar covers daytime use directly; leftover solar charges the battery, which covers evening use up to its usable size. Surplus is not credited (no feed-in). Peak use is estimated from the average load (monthly kWh ÷ 730 hours) as 1.5 kW plus 2.8 × the average, ±20%: a typical home's evening peak with a geyser, stove and kettle. A site assessment measures the real figure. The grid bill is re-priced on what is left, then the Versofy fee is added. The recommendation follows Versofy's published guide: the package with the highest "suitable for bills above" level the bill reaches, from those that suit the home's supply (three-phase homes: PLUS 3P or SUPERCHARGE). While a limited deal runs (to the date in its note), it is recommended from its own level upwards. Below every level, it picks the lowest-cost package. If another package gives a clearly bigger estimated saving, the calculator says so. Sales can change the package at any time.

Long term: each year the model is re-run with panel output and battery capacity reduced by the ageing rates set in admin (default 0.5% and 2.5% a year), and the grid saving rises with the yearly tariff increase; the fee rises with the subscription increase. Years 1 to 10 are saving minus fee. The contract ends after year 10 and the customer can buy the system for the buy-out price (default R1); from year 11 the whole grid saving counts. The inverter is out of warranty by then but assumed to keep working.

What it leaves out

  • Winter output is roughly 25–30% below summer; figures are year-round averages.
  • The value of backup power. When the monthly figure is negative, it is shown as the price of backup instead.
  • After year 10: upkeep and a battery replacement (batteries often last 10 to 15 years) are the customer's cost.
  • Maintenance, insurance and monitoring in the subscription.
  • Indigent, lifeline and time-of-use tariffs.

Disclaimer

This is an estimate, not a quote, offer or guarantee. It is based only on what the customer told us (monthly spend, area, supplier, meter type and supply), which we have not checked.

It uses a simplified model: year-round average solar output, an assumed daytime share of use and fixed yearly increases. Actual savings and backup time will differ with weather, how and when power is used, outages, the installation, the home's wiring and changes to tariffs or regulation. Versofy does not guarantee any saving, backup time or payback. Long-term figures are illustrations on the stated assumptions, not forecasts. This is not financial advice.

Versofy does not determine which tariff the customer is on. We use the tariff a standalone home with this supplier is most likely to be on, at published 2026/27 rates incl. VAT, and cannot confirm that it matches the customer's own tariff or bill.

Prices are as at the date shown and may change; after 30 days, ask us for an updated estimate. This estimate does not form part of any agreement: only a site assessment and the signed Versofy subscription agreement, with its terms and conditions, set the final system, price and obligations, and they take precedence over this document. The customer's details are used only to prepare this estimate and follow up on it, in line with the Protection of Personal Information Act and Versofy's privacy policy.

The suburb list is a guide, so confirm the supplier with the customer. Nelson Mandela Bay rates are proposed and under dispute; Centlec's are averaged over summer and winter with VAT added; one source lists a R142.50 Tshwane basic charge that others don't.
