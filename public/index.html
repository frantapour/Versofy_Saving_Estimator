How the saving is worked out

Monthly kWh is backed out of the spend through the tariff's price blocks. Solar covers daytime use directly; leftover solar charges the battery, which covers evening use up to its usable size. Surplus is not credited (no feed-in). Peak use is estimated from the average load (monthly kWh ÷ 730 hours) as 1.5 kW plus 2.8 × the average, ±20%: a typical home's evening peak with a geyser, stove and kettle. A site assessment measures the real figure. The grid bill is re-priced on what is left, then the Versofy fee is added. The recommendation is the package with the best net monthly saving (grid bill reduction minus the Versofy fee) of all packages that suit the home's supply.

Long term: the grid saving rises with the yearly tariff increase, less 0.5% a year for panel ageing; the fee rises with the subscription increase. Years 1 to 10 are saving minus fee. From year 11 the subscription ends and the customer keeps the system, so the whole grid saving counts.

What it leaves out

  • Winter output is roughly 25–30% below summer; figures are year-round averages.
  • The value of backup power. When the monthly figure is negative, it is shown as the price of backup instead.
  • After year 10: upkeep and a battery replacement (batteries often last 10 to 15 years) are the customer's cost.
  • Maintenance, insurance and monitoring in the subscription.
  • Indigent, lifeline and time-of-use tariffs.

Disclaimer

This is an estimate, not a quote or an offer. It is based only on the information the customer gave us: their average monthly electricity spend, suburb or town, electricity supplier and type of supply. We have not checked this information.

The figures come from a simplified model. It uses year-round average solar output, an assumed daytime share of use and fixed yearly increases, so actual savings will vary with weather, usage, the installation and future tariff changes.

Versofy does not determine which tariff the customer is on. The calculator uses the tariff a standalone home with this supplier is most likely to be on, at published 2026/27 rates incl. VAT. We cannot confirm that these rates match the customer's own tariff or bill, and the results will differ if they do not.

Final figures depend on a site assessment and the signed Versofy subscription agreement.

The suburb list is a guide, so confirm the supplier with the customer. Nelson Mandela Bay rates are proposed and under dispute; Centlec's are averaged over summer and winter with VAT added; one source lists a R142.50 Tshwane basic charge that others don't.
